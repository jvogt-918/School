#Excercise 1 A. Briefly explain the bias variance trade off as a concept.

Effectively its almost like a see-saw, on one side there is bias and the other has variance. As you make your model more complex to the dataset, it effectively conforms to the dataset and creates more variance to it. If you make your model TOO simple, than the model may have too much bias. The key is to find the balance inbetween the two to level out the “seesaw”

With a very low knn such as 1, the nearest neight is usually a datapoint extremely simiarl to itself, this could create overfitting and low bias, but higher variaance.

The “lambda” controls how much effect each predictor has on the model. If the penalty is VERY large, than the effect of each predictor becomes very low and such a model would likley to have very little to no predictors on it.

D. The feature selection tool is somewhat automated. You feed it the response and predictors, and it does the rest. Using your previous answers, write a short explanation to a new analyst on why feature selection tools are not a fix-all to the entire modeling process.

Feature Selection tools run calculations based solely on numbers. They use tests and penalties and model perfomrance metrics to determine which predictors should be dropped and kept. The problem with these tools is that they do not have domain knowledge nor do they have any true meaning of what the data means. The tools don’t capture whether the data was recorded properly or even if the data are correlated through some non-quantitatve factor. Although these feature selection tools are are VERY helpful, they are not a be-all or end-all tool, they should be complimented with a skillfull analyst along with someone that have domain knowledge in the data that you’re measuring/predicting.
